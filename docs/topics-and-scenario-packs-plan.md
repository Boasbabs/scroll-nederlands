# Topics tab and scenario packs: plan

Adds real-life situations and a way to practise vocabulary by topic, after a review of Dutched (the *Dutch in 3 Months* PDF bundle and the chapters at access.dutched.app) against TaalNudge. Agreed with the owner on 2026-10-29: scenario packs beside the course, a bigger word track, a fifth tab called Topics, and A1–A2 content before any B1 work.

## What the review found

### Dutched in brief

- **The app** has 17 chapters, each one a word list with articles, pronunciation, audio, category filters and a "known" mark, plus 15–25 ready-made phrases, three dialogues, a grammar and culture page and a 10-question quiz. Chapters 1–4 (sounds, numbers and time, greetings, basic grammar) overlap TaalNudge's Stage 0 and Stage 1. Chapters 5–17 hold the real-life topics: gemeente, housing, banking, transport, supermarket, health, work, going out, shopping and services, emergencies, false friends, advanced grammar and culture. Together they list 1,010 words.
- **Practice** under Quiz & Games, per chapter or mixed: self-rated flashcards, multiple choice, match the pairs, listen and choose, and choose the missing word. All of them test recognition only. A **60-Day Path** lists real-world tasks to tick off ("order a coffee in Dutch", "introduce yourself to a neighbour").
- ***Dutch in 3 Months*** is a grammar-first course in nine weeks (A0–A2) with a six-line dialogue each week. Its real-life material sits at the back: four model dialogues (GP, gemeente, restaurant, work), nine graded readings, a sentence bank by theme and starter word lists. **Vol 2** applies the same method from A2 to B2. The **Survival Phrasebook** has 15 situations, two of which the app lacks: children and school, and post and internet. The bundled **dictionary** is a published frequency dictionary.

*Taught* means the word is a course item or a word-track word, or appears inside a taught phrase. *Seen only* means it appears in examples, dialogues or exercises but is never an item. Entries the app lists twice were merged, so a few chapters show fewer than 80.

TaalNudge already has lessons in most of these situations: s3-service (registering at the gemeente, sending a parcel, at the GP), s2-health (calling the GP, the pharmacy, emergencies), s2-home (a message to your landlord), s3-work (calling in sick), s2-travel and s2-shopissues. What it lacks is depth. The words around each situation are missing (borg, servicekosten, salaris, korting, recept, verzekering, aangifte), and so are whole areas: money and bills, job contracts and job hunting, police and insurance, school, and customs and holidays. Not every absent word is worth teaching, though. The 538 include expat niche terms such as 30%-regeling, erfpacht, kwartaalbelasting and rekening-courant. The packs below keep 20–30 high-need items per situation.

### What TaalNudge already does better (keep it)

- Dutched tests recognition only; TaalNudge climbs from recognition through supported and independent recall to transfer, with typed answers and short writing.
- Dutched's flashcards are self-rated, with no schedule. TaalNudge schedules reviews and flags leeches.
- TaalNudge also has the de/het drill and het mnemonic, pictures and personal notes, minimal-pair ear training, the corrections loop and in-page nudges.

The gap is content breadth, not method. Nothing below changes how TaalNudge teaches.

### Dutched's content problems (why we copy nothing)

- **Wrong articles:** het lekkage, de incassobureau, de bankafschrift, de OV-abonnement, het Wegenwacht, de statiegeld, de tasje, het vleeswaren, de functioneringsgesprek, het Ziektewet, de UWV, het thee, het coffeeshop, de kaasplankje, het stamppot, de prijskaartje, de pakketpunt, de schadeformulier, het rechtbank, de Rijksmuseum, de Anne Frank huis.
- **Errors:** "kentekening" (kenteken), "gardering" (garantie), "spielend" (German; Dutch is spelend), "dat is niet mijn problem", a flat "te huur voor negentig euro per maand".
- **Padding:** brand and site names counted as words (ING, Bunq, Funda, Thuisbezorgd.nl), English notes listed as entries, grammar labels listed as vocabulary.
- **False Friends chapter:** many entries are not false friends at all ("rood, not rude", "koud, not could", "oud, not out"), some are wrong ("de zone = son", "de star", "de zwijg"), and the classic traps for English speakers are missing (eventueel, actueel, brutaal, raar, room, wet, kind, door).
- **Rights:** the chapters, dialogues and PDFs are Dutched's paid content, and the dictionary is a published book. Use them as a checklist of topics, the same way the 625 list treats Wyner's list. Write our own items, texts and dialogues.

### Grammar against TaalNudge

These A2 patterns come up in real situations, but TaalNudge doesn't yet teach them as patterns. The packs teach them as chunks, with no new grammar modules.

| Pattern | Real-life example | TaalNudge today | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reflexive verbs (me, je, zich) | Ik wil me inschrijven. Ik voel me niet lekker. | a few phrases (s2-health, s3-service) | Gemeente, Doctor, Work |
| Object pronouns (hem, haar, het, ze) | Kunt u het me sturen? Wie haalt hem op? | seen in 22 lessons, never taught | Services, School |
| te + infinitive | Vergeet niet uit te checken. | 1 lesson | Getting around, Gemeente |
| aan het + infinitive | Ik ben aan het koken, ik bel je terug. | absent | Neighbours, Services |
| zou for politeness | Zou u dat willen herhalen? | 9 lessons | Services |
| Passive, recognition only | Uw aanvraag wordt behandeld. | absent | letters and notices in Gemeente, Work, Services |
| Relative die/dat, recognition only | de brief die u heeft ontvangen | absent | Gemeente letters |

The rest of Vol 2 is B1 and can wait: the imperfect for storytelling, subordinate clauses in depth, er-compounds, producing the passive, and conditionals.

## Ground rules

- **Leave the 272 lessons alone.** The Fluent Forever plan's rule holds: no edits to `src/curriculum/content/**`, to lesson order or to lesson and item ids. Packs and the Topics tab sit beside the path; they never insert lessons into it.
- **Local only.** No network at runtime, no new host permissions, no analytics.
- **Permanent ids.** Pack scene lessons are `sc-<pack>-<scene>` and their items are `sc-<pack>-<scene>/<key>`. Tier-2 words keep the word track's `w-<theme>/<key>`. The prefixes `w-`, `p-` and `c-` are taken.
- **One home per word.** A word is taught once: in the course, the word track or one pack. Anywhere else, exercises refer to it by its full item id, which the exercise model already allows.
- **Original content.** Dutched is a checklist only. Check every article and spelling against woordenlijst.org, and include the packs in the NT2 review the README asks for.
- **Situations, not categories.** A pack is a situation (renting a flat), never a taxonomy (ten fruits). See "Evidence".
- **Audio:** the browser's nl-NL voice reads anything without a clip until the ElevenLabs quota resets.
- **Every phase:** unit and UI tests, `pnpm compile`, `pnpm test`, `pnpm build`, and a look in the preview harness. Update the status table when a phase lands.
- **Git:** commit only when the owner asks. **Handoff:** at 55% context, update this file's status and continue in a new max-effort session.

## Phase T2: pack engine

A pack is a short list of **scene lessons** that reuse `LessonDraft` and the lesson player as they are, plus a pack-level mission and culture note. No new exercise kinds are needed.

```ts
// src/packs/types.ts
export type Pack = {
  id: string;                    // kebab-case: 'huren'
  title: string;                 // 'Renting a home'
  situation: string;             // one English line for the pack screen
  buildsOn: Array<string>;       // course module ids, e.g. ['s2-home']
  scenes: Array<LessonDraft>;    // 3–4 scenes, ids 'sc-huren-<scene>'
  mission: string;               // "Do it for real": learner-led, never assessed
  cultureNote?: string;          // at most two sentences
};
```

- **Index:** `indexCurriculum(source, wordThemes, contrasts, packs)` adds the scene lessons and their items. `stageId` is `'packs'` and `moduleId` is `sc-<pack>`, so generated drills draw wrong options from the same pack. `lessons` and the path stay course-only, the same way the word track is kept out.
- **Selection:** scenes unlock in order within a pack. A pack never waits on the path; the screen shows "Best after: Stage 1 · Home" from `buildsOn` but does not lock. Starting a scene runs the existing lesson flow in focused mode. Introduced pack items enter reviews, and so the in-page nudges, like any other item. Packs never pre-empt due reviews.
- **Pack screen:** the scenes (start, continue, done), the pack's items with status dots, **Practise this pack** (`startPractice` over the pack's learned items plus the course items of `buildsOn` modules), the mission with a "Done it" tick, and the culture note. The tick is stored locally in its own key; it is not scored.
- **Today:** when a pack is in progress, show a row "Continue: Renting a home · scene 2 of 4" under New words.
- **Progress:** add "Situations: 3 of 13 started", with items learned out of the total.
- **Validator** (`tests/packs/packs.test.ts`):
  - ids are unique and namespaced;
  - no item duplicates a course item, a word-track word or another pack's item, ignoring the article;
  - nouns carry de or het;
  - each pack has 3–4 scenes, at least 20 items in total, at least one `listenOnly` exercise, one `passage` exercise and one `write` exercise, and a mission;
  - each scene follows the core and practice lesson rules (one exercise per stage, in order).

### As built (2026-10-29)

- **Data:** `src/packs/types.ts` as specified; `src/packs/build.ts` has `noun`, `word`, `phrase` and `pattern` helpers. A noun's bare form is accepted when typed, as course nouns allow, since the de/het drill asks the article apart. Content lives in `src/packs/content/<id>.ts`, listed in `content/index.ts`.
- **Index:** `indexCurriculum(…, packs)` places the scenes (`src/packs/assemble.ts`, `PACKS_STAGE`, module `sc-<pack>`, level a1-a2, `order` = the scene's place in its pack) as `CurriculumIndex.packs`. `lesson()` finds scenes; `lessons`, the path and the course counts stay course-only. Pack items join `items`, and singular nouns get de/het items; `LearningItemDraft` gained `plural` for nouns like "de servicekosten".
- **Selection:** `isLessonUnlocked` sends scenes to `isSceneUnlocked` (in order within the pack). `packOfScene`, `getNextScene`, `packActivity`, `getPackInProgress` (the most recently active unfinished pack, for Today).
- **One rule beyond the plan:** a scene may practise course or word-track items by full id before the learner has met them, since a pack never waits on the path. `completeLesson` leaves those for the course to introduce, so a pack never schedules a word the learner hasn't met in its own lesson. Items already met are graded like any revisit.
- **Reviews** draw on the scenes' authored exercises as they do on lessons' (`authoredExercisesFor`).
- **Actions:** `startLesson` plays a scene; `startNextScene(packId)`, `startPackPractice(packId)` (the pack's items, the course items of its `buildsOn` modules and their de/het items, due first) and `setMission(packId, done)`. - **Validator:** `src/packs/validate.ts`, run by `tests/packs/packs.test.ts`, with the plan's checks plus: pack ids never equal a word theme's (the Topics tab opens either by id), `buildsOn` names real modules, the culture note has one or two sentences, a noun's English starts with "the" (and only a noun's), plurals take de, no prerequisites (scenes open in order), and an exercise may refer to its own scene, an earlier one, the course, the word track or another pack, never a later scene.

## Pack format

Each pack has these parts:

| Part | What | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| Items | 20–30, about one third chunks and patterns (*Ik wil me inschrijven op …*) and two thirds words | `items` |
| Dialogue | 6–10 lines in one scene | `dialogue`, `context` |
| A real-life text | a letter, advert, label, payslip, notification or notice, 40–90 words, with 2–3 questions | `passage` + `choice` |
| Listening | a phone call, voicemail, phone menu or announcement, heard before the text is shown | `audioText` + `listenOnly` |
| Writing | a reply, request or complaint of 15–40 words, checked leniently and sent through the corrections loop | `write` with `mustInclude` |
| Mission | one real task in the learner's own life | `mission` |
| Culture note | what a newcomer gets wrong, in at most two sentences | `cultureNote` |

The exercise ladder is the same as in the course. Recognition: match, choose, listen and choose. Supported recall: cloze with a hint, order the tiles. Independent recall: type from the English or from a situation. Transfer: read the text and answer, reply in writing.

## Worked example: Gemeente and paperwork

This pack sets the standard for the others. Every line here is original, and every one should still get the Dutch-speaker check.

**Scene 1: At the counter** (`sc-gemeente-loket`)

| Dutch | English | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| Ik wil me inschrijven op dit adres. | I'd like to register at this address. | pattern (reflexive) |
| Wat moet ik meenemen? | What do I need to bring? | phrase |
| het loket | counter | word |
| het nummertje | queue ticket | word; een nummertje trekken |
| de ambtenaar | civil servant | word |
| het identiteitsbewijs | proof of identity | word |
| de verblijfsvergunning | residence permit | word |
| het BSN | citizen service number | word; het burgerservicenummer |

Dialogue:

> **Medewerker:** Goedemorgen. Heeft u een afspraak?
> **U:** Ja, om tien uur. Ik wil me inschrijven op mijn nieuwe adres.
> **Medewerker:** Mag ik uw identiteitsbewijs?
> **U:** Hier is mijn paspoort. En dit is mijn huurcontract.
> **Medewerker:** Dank u. Wilt u dit formulier invullen en hier ondertekenen?
> **U:** Natuurlijk. Wanneer krijg ik mijn BSN?
> **Medewerker:** Dat krijgt u per brief, op dit adres.
> **U:** Prima. Dank u wel.

**Scene 2: Forms and a letter about your move** (`sc-gemeente-formulier`): de achternaam, de geboorteplaats, de burgerlijke staat (gehuwd, ongehuwd), ondertekenen, de handtekening, de adreswijziging. The course already teaches de voornaam, de geboortedatum, de bijlage and uiterlijk, so the scene's exercises refer to those by id. The form fields are cloze exercises over a printed form (`Achternaam: ___` → *Okafor*). The scene ends with this letter and an email back:

> **Gemeente Dijkstad** · Onderwerp: uw verhuizing
> Beste mevrouw Okafor,
> Wij hebben uw verhuizing ontvangen. U staat vanaf 3 maart ingeschreven op Kanaalstraat 12. Klopt dit adres niet? Bel ons dan uiterlijk 17 maart.
> Met vriendelijke groet,
> Afdeling Burgerzaken

Transfer: email the gemeente that you moved on 1 March, give the new address and ask when you will get a confirmation (`mustInclude`: verhuisd, adres, wanneer).

**Scene 3: DigiD and allowances** (`sc-gemeente-digid`): DigiD (*inloggen met je DigiD*), aanvragen, de aanvraag, de toeslag, de zorgtoeslag, and *Is er een tolk?* The listening text (`audioText` with `listenOnly`):

> Welkom bij de gemeente Dijkstad. Voor paspoorten en id-kaarten, toets 1. Voor verhuizen en inschrijven, toets 2. Voor afval, toets 3. Voor alle andere vragen, blijft u aan de lijn.

**Scene 4: A letter about your application** (`sc-gemeente-besluit`): in behandeling, het besluit, de termijn, goedgekeurd, afgewezen, bezwaar maken, and the patterns *Ik heb een brief gekregen die ik niet begrijp.* and *Mijn aanvraag is nog in behandeling.* The text (the exercise's `passage`):

> **Dienst Toeslagen** · Onderwerp: uw aanvraag zorgtoeslag
> Beste mevrouw Okafor,
> Wij hebben uw aanvraag ontvangen. Uw aanvraag is nu in behandeling. U krijgt zo snel mogelijk een besluit. Bent u het niet eens met het besluit? Dan kunt u bezwaar maken.
> Met vriendelijke groet,
> Dienst Toeslagen

Exercises for scene 4, one per stage, in the shape the validator expects:

```ts
// recognition: read the letter, pick the meaning
{ id: 'rec', stage: 'recognition', kind: 'choice', passage: LETTER, items: ['in-behandeling'],
  prompt: 'What is happening to your application now?',
  options: ['It is being processed', 'It has been approved', 'It has been rejected'], answer: 'It is being processed',
  explanation: '"in behandeling" means someone is dealing with it; there is no decision yet.' },
// supported recall: the objection
{ id: 'cloze', stage: 'supportedRecall', kind: 'cloze', items: ['bezwaar-maken'],
  sentence: 'Bent u het niet eens met het besluit? Dan kunt u ___ maken.', answers: ['bezwaar'], hint: 'objection',
  english: 'Do you disagree with the decision? Then you can object.', explanation: 'bezwaar maken: to object to an official decision.' },
// independent recall: say it yourself
{ id: 'type', stage: 'independentRecall', kind: 'type', items: ['brief-niet-begrijpen'],
  prompt: 'At the counter, say: I got a letter that I don\'t understand.',
  answers: ['Ik heb een brief gekregen die ik niet begrijp.'],
  explanation: '"die" points back to "de brief"; the verb goes to the end.' },
// transfer: object in writing
{ id: 'write', stage: 'transfer', kind: 'write', minWords: 10, items: ['bezwaar-maken'],
  prompt: 'Your application was rejected. Write that you disagree with the decision and want to object.',
  mustInclude: [['eens', 'oneens'], ['bezwaar']],
  modelAnswer: 'Ik ben het niet eens met het besluit. Ik wil bezwaar maken.',
  explanation: '"het niet eens zijn met" plus the decision, then "bezwaar maken".' },
```

**Mission:** find the "verhuizen" page on your gemeente's website and read the list of what to bring, in Dutch. **Culture note:** you must report a move to the gemeente no later than five days after it, and your BSN is needed for work, the bank and the GP. Allowances (toeslagen) come from Dienst Toeslagen, part of the tax office, not from the gemeente.

## Phase T6: word track tier 2 (words 626–1,000)

Wyner's next step after the 625 basic words is the 1,000 most frequent words. These are general words used in every situation, so they belong in the word track, not in a pack.

- **Data:** a `tier: 1 | 2` field on `WordTheme`. Tier 1 stays exactly 625 and tier 2 is exactly 375. Progress shows "Core words: N of 1,000".
- **Selection:** tier 2 starts when 90% of tier 1 has been introduced, or straight away for a theme the learner picks in Topics.
- **Choosing the words:** rank with SUBTLEX-NL, leaving out anything taught in the course, tier 1 or a pack. Picture-able words get pictures. Abstract words are learned from a sentence with a gap, Wyner's method for words you can't picture, using the existing cloze exercise.
- **Themes, with samples that are in neither the course nor tier 1:**
  - everyday abstract nouns: het probleem, de reden, het idee, de keuze, de kans, de fout, de manier, het deel, de regel, de informatie, de mening, het verschil, de oplossing;
  - feelings: zenuwachtig, trots, jaloers, eenzaam, verbaasd, teleurgesteld, tevreden, ongerust, gestrest, verliefd;

## Evidence behind the choices

- **Situations, not categories.** Learning new words in semantic sets (colours, fruit, body parts) slows learning through interference. Thematic sets built around a situation do not have that problem (Tinkham 1993, 1997; Waring 1997; Erten & Tekin 2008; Nation 2000). Packs are therefore situations, the Today row keeps mixing themes, and "Learn new" in a single word theme is limited to one small batch a day.
- **Recall over recognition.** Retrieving a word makes it stick far better than recognising it again (Karpicke & Roediger 2008). The packs keep TaalNudge's ladder up to typed recall and writing instead of Dutched's choose-the-answer games.
- **Spacing.** Spreading out reviews beats massing them (Cepeda et al. 2006). Topic practice goes through the existing scheduler and never pushes a review out early.
- **Chunks.** Fixed phrases make learners sound more fluent and are easier to reuse (Boers et al. 2006), so about a third of each pack is chunks.
- **Narrow reading.** Several short texts on one topic recycle its vocabulary (Krashen 2004). Each pack therefore has its own text, dialogue and listening task on the same situation.
- **Missions.** A concrete "when and where I'll do it" plan raises follow-through (Gollwitzer 1999). This is the useful idea in Dutched's 60-Day Path, kept learner-led and unscored.
- **Fluent Forever.** Personal relevance and pictures (Wyner 2014). The Topics tab lets learners choose the situations their own life needs first.
