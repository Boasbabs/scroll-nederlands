# Dutch audio: voices, model and settings

How TaalNudge's pre-generated Dutch clips (A0 to A2, standard Netherlands Dutch, nl-NL) are voiced, made and checked.

**Status: Google Chirp 3: HD; every clip made on 29 October 2026.** Every one of the course's 5,366 texts has a clip. Main voice (a): Erinome. Dialogue voice (b): Algieba. Pace 1. `scripts/generate-audio.ts` produces the clips; `docs/audio-qa.md` lists the clips its check flags. The voice tests and the ElevenLabs research behind these choices are in this file's git history.

## Decision (29 October)

- **Provider: Google Cloud Text-to-Speech, Chirp 3: HD.** On the course's own texts the best Google voices were as accurate as ElevenLabs, the course's first provider. The whole course, about 152,000 characters, fits in Chirp 3: HD's free million characters a month, where ElevenLabs needed about 125,000 credits, two months of its Starter plan. Chirp 3: HD also has a pace setting and custom pronunciations, which ElevenLabs' `eleven_v4` lacks.
- **Main voice (a): Erinome, `nl-NL-Chirp3-HD-Erinome` (female), the owner's choice.** She reads everything except the second speaker of a dialogue. On the 20 test texts both providers had, she matched ElevenLabs: 13 of 14 short texts back exactly, 6 of 6 sentences, no silent take.
- **Dialogue voice (b): Algieba, `nl-NL-Chirp3-HD-Algieba` (male).** He reads the second speaker in a conversation (378 texts), so dialogues keep a female and a male voice. He matched ElevenLabs on the same texts and went 64 test takes without a silent one.
- **Settings:** `speakingRate` 1. At 0.9 the check heard every voice worse. Chirp 3: HD has no model setting (the voice name picks it), so `chirp3-hd` names it in the manifest and the cache keys.
- **Silent takes.** For a one-word text, Google sometimes returns about 0.28 s of near silence (-43 to -61 dB), at random. Some takes peak just above -50 dB, survive the trimming, and loudness normalisation would turn them into noise. The pipeline measures each raw take's peak before post-processing. Under -30 dB it asks again, five tries in all. A single word that stays silent then goes as a sentence ("ja" as "Ja."), five tries more, since Google voices a written word far more reliably. Only after that is the text left to the browser's voice and listed in `docs/audio-qa.md`.
- **Spelling.** Every Google voice reads letters joined by hyphens ("Smith. S-M-I-T-H.") as the word, but spells letters set apart by commas. The course's three hyphen-spelled names are sent as "S, M, I, T, H."; the learner sees, and the manifest keys, the text as written. The other spelling lines already use commas ("Ik spel het: E, V, A.").
- **Output:** Google returns LINEAR16 WAV at 24 kHz. The master is that take converted losslessly to FLAC. Then ffmpeg:
  - trim silence to 50 ms before and 150 ms after;
  - two-pass `loudnorm` to -18 LUFS, -1.5 dBTP;
  - encode mono Opus at 32 kbps and 48 kHz in Ogg (about 4 KB per second), bitexact, so the same master always makes the same file.
- **One voice per part.** A run keeps a part's clips from before only if the same model and voice made them, so a new voice remakes its whole part and no part mixes two voices.
- **Slow replay:** `playbackRate` (`SLOW_RATE` 0.7 in `src/audio/speech.ts`, pitch preserved). There are no separate slow clips.

## Setup

A Google Cloud project with billing linked, the Cloud Text-to-Speech and Cloud Speech-to-Text APIs enabled, and an API key restricted to those two, in `.env.local` as `GOOGLE_CLOUD_API_KEY`. `GOOGLE_TTS_ENDPOINT=https://eu-texttospeech.googleapis.com` in `.env.local` keeps the Text-to-Speech calls in the EU, where the course was voiced. The pipeline also needs ffmpeg and ffprobe on the PATH.

## Generation pipeline (`scripts/generate-audio.ts`)

1. **Texts.** The script builds the course from `structure.ts` and `content/**`, then the scenario packs' scenes, plus the word track and the minimal pairs, in this priority order: lesson `targetPhrase`, `items`, the word track's new words and their sentences, the pairs' words, exercise `audioText`, exercise `context`, lesson `dialogue`, `examples`, then model answers. Model answers are the cloze sentence with `answers[0]` filled in, the `order` words joined, the `type` `answers[0]`, the `write` `modelAnswer`, and the Dutch side of `match` pairs. A text used in several places belongs to its first group. In a conversation, the second speaker reads with voice b (`clipsB`); further speakers alternate.
2. **Masters, cache keys and file names.** Every take that speaks is kept as a master, `audio-masters/<16 hex>.flac` (gitignored; `AUDIO_MASTERS_DIR` moves it), keyed by `sha1(what was sent, voice, model, settings)`: a word sent as a sentence is keyed by the sentence, and the planner looks for each form in turn. Google's takes differ each time, so the master is the take the check heard, and a retake replaces it. A clip's key adds the post-processing version: `audio/<16 hex>.ogg`. A clip missing from `public/` is made from its master when there is one; only a text with neither calls the API. A text keeps the clip it has until the run makes its own, as long as the same model and voice made it. The manifest `src/audio/clips.json` holds `{ voices, model, settings, clips, clipsB }`, keyed by `clipKey(text)`. It is rebuilt from the current course on every run, so stale entries drop out, and it is saved after every clip.
3. **Requests** (`scripts/google-cloud.ts`).
   - Three in parallel. The key goes only in the `X-Goog-Api-Key` header. `GOOGLE_TTS_ENDPOINT` (the EU endpoint, set in `.env.local`) and `GOOGLE_STT_ENDPOINT` pick regional endpoints.
   - A per-minute limit (429) or a server error is retried with backoff, for about a minute in all.
   - A refused key or project, a spent daily or monthly quota, or a limit that outlasts the retries stops the run cleanly. Running the same command again resumes.
   - `--plan` counts texts and characters without calling the API; `--encode` makes clips from the masters only, also without the API; `--until <group>` and `--max-chars N` cap a run; `--report` rewrites `docs/audio-qa.md` from the stored checks; `--prune` deletes clips the manifest no longer uses.
4. **Verification, group by group.** Google Speech-to-Text v1 (FLAC at 16 kHz, Dutch (nl-NL) forced) checks every text of up to 3 words plus a fixed 10% sample of longer ones. It compares the words in lower case, without punctuation or accents, with numbers and clock times spelled out and "ij" equal to "ei". A clip is flagged when a short text is not exact, when a longer one has WER > 0.2, or when its length does not fit the text. A flagged clip is made again once (another take) and checked again. Clips still flagged go to `docs/audio-qa.md` as a listening list, together with any text that has no clip. If Speech-to-Text refuses its calls, the run goes on making clips, unchecked.

## Generation status, size and cost (29 October)

- **The run.** `pnpm audio:generate` voiced 5,365 of the 5,366 texts in 34 minutes: 4,987 with Erinome, 378 with Algieba. 16 takes came back silent and were asked for again. Only "ja" never spoke: Erinome returned silence on all five tries, and on five more in the `--prune` run. That led to the sentence fallback: sent as "Ja.", it spoke on its fourth try, and Speech-to-Text heard "ja". Every text now has a clip: 4,988 for Erinome, 378 for Algieba.
- **Characters billed:** 153,562 in the run, retakes and silent takes included, and about 6,500 more in the tests and small runs around it. That is about 16% of Chirp 3: HD's free million for the month, so there is no charge.
- **The check.** Speech-to-Text checked 2,464 clips (2,160 short, 304 longer). 203 were flagged on the first take and made again; 118 are still flagged and listed in `docs/audio-qa.md` as a listening list. Most are one-syllable words heard as nothing or as a near word (24 came back empty, isolated "maan" among them; "man" passed this time), clock times written as digits ("tien over drie" as "3:10"), and names ("Sharma" heard as "shoarma", its letters right). A spot check of 12 clips the run had not checked, from both voices, heard 11 within one word in five; the twelfth was the street name "Lindelaan", heard as "Lindenlaan".
- **Speech-to-Text cost.** 2,667 calls with about 53 minutes of audio, billed by the second. The first 60 minutes a month are free (the tests before the run used some of them), then $0.016 to $0.024 a minute: under a dollar of the trial credit.
- **Size.** The clips take 42.5 MB in the built extension (`dist/chrome-mv3/audio`), against the 50 MB estimated; the whole build is 47.8 MB. The FLAC masters take 310 MB in `audio-masters/`, with the checks in `checks.json` beside them: back that folder up.
- **The first clips.** The course's first 1,270 clips came from ElevenLabs (Serge de Beer and Roos on `eleven_v4`) until its monthly quota ran out. They left the manifest when the Google run began, and `pnpm audio:generate --prune` deleted their MP3s afterwards. Among them was the "Dank je wel!" clip that the launch video's soundtrack fetches; it stays in git history (commit 0eb8821) for a re-render.

## Limitations

- **STT measures intelligibility, not accent.** It cannot tell nl-NL from nl-BE, and it mishears isolated sounds and one-syllable words (isolated "man" and "maan" for every voice), so the QA list is a listening list. Google's recogniser may also hear Google's own voices more easily. The Netherlands accent rests on Google's labels (both voices are nl-NL), not on a native listener. A Dutch speaker should spot-check about 20 clips per voice: the hard g/sch, ui/eu/ij, w, v/z devoicing and r.
- **Google's takes cannot be made again.** The same text gives a slightly different take each time, so `audio-masters/` holds the only copy of the checked takes. A lost master is voiced again, as a new take, only when its clip has to be remade, and is then checked like any new take.
- **Chirp 3: HD may change.** Google can update its voices, so clips made in later months may differ slightly in delivery.
